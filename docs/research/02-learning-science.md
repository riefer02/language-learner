# Learning Science Brief for Kiku

*Scope: evidence through 2026. Effect sizes are Hedges' g unless noted. "Strong" = replicated meta-analysis; "moderate" = mixed or context-dependent; "inference" = a design judgment, not a direct finding. Kiku is assumed to be a mobile-first Korean app combining SRS, graded input, pronunciation training, and conversation practice.*

## 1. Memory and neuroplasticity

**Principle.** Memory is reconstructive, not recorded. A trace is stabilized by consolidation, strengthened by effortful retrieval, and reorganized by sleep. Adult L2 learning changes brain structure and function; the "critical period" is better modeled as a sensitive period with large individual variation.

**Evidence.** Synaptic consolidation stabilizes new traces within hours; systems consolidation redistributes them to neocortex over days to weeks, with hippocampal–cortical replay concentrated in NREM sleep. Sleep spindles correlate with declarative memory (r ≈ .23; [Chen, Hao & Ma, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/18344909241226761)), and slow-oscillation–spindle coupling predicts retention ([Ng, Noh & Spencer, 2024](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.101992.1)). Cueing memories during NREM improves recall (g = 0.29; [Hu et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7144680/)), but this is a laboratory manipulation, not "sleep learning." Intensive adult L2 training produces grey-matter and myelinated white-matter changes within 3–6 months, correlated with proficiency ([Wei et al., 2024](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306286121)); plasticity persists across the lifespan ([van Hell, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12601)). Age of acquisition mainly affects ultimate attainment and native-like processing, not whether learning is possible ([Birdsong, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5857581/)).

**Design implication.** Make every encounter a retrieval event. Put new material earlier in the day and protect sleep; offer a 2-minute bedtime recall as a cue, never as new learning. Do not market "sleep learning." Frame adult learning as slow but real: 20–30 minutes/day for 6+ months produces measurable change. Avoid childhood-determinism copy.

## 2. Retrieval, spacing, and scheduling

**Principle.** Retrieval practice and spacing are the most robust, cheapest interventions available. So-called "desirable difficulties"—testing, spacing, interleaving, generation—help only when the learner can eventually succeed with feedback.

**Evidence.** Practice testing beats restudy by g = 0.61 across 272 effect sizes ([Adesope et al., 2017](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2017-adesope.pdf)); classroom studies find 57% medium-to-large effects ([Yang et al., 2021](https://remix.berklee.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1008&context=faculty-works)). Smaller, interspersed tests beat end-of-unit tests (g = 0.25; [Don et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1037/xlm0001382)). Spaced versus massed retrieval yields g = 0.74, but expanding versus uniform intervals yields only g = 0.03 ([Latimier, Peyre & Ramus, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8)). Optimal gaps scale with the retention interval: ~3 days for a 7-day test, ~27 days for a 1-year test, i.e., 5–10% of the interval at long delays ([Cepeda et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)). The "85% rule" derives a 15.87% error optimum for gradient-descent binary classification ([Wilson et al., 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4)); treat it as a difficulty heuristic, not a universal law.

**Algorithms.** Leitner boxes use fixed intervals and no item difficulty: transparent but inefficient. SM-2 stores one ease factor (minimum 1.3), schedules 1 day, 6 days, then × ease, and cannot separate intrinsic difficulty from memory strength. FSRS-6 models difficulty, stability, and retrievability with 21 parameters and a power forgetting curve, trained on the learner's own log. In a 350M-review benchmark it beat SM-2 on log loss for 99.6% of users; project simulations report 20–30% fewer reviews at equal retention ([Expertium benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html); [FSRS history](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/G7fpGCi8r7nCKXsQk/the-history-of-fsrs-for-anki)). FSRS needs several hundred reviews to personalize; cold start should use population defaults. Desired retention of 0.90 is the default; above 0.97 workload rises steeply ([Anki manual summary](https://j-compass.com/docs/japanese-learning/study-methodology/spaced-repetition/sm2-fsrs-algorithms)).

**Design implication.** Default Kiku to FSRS-6 with four-grade recall, desired retention 0.90, and a visible review-load forecast. Review before new material. Cap new items at 5–10/day; offer a "breadth" mode at 0.85 retention with a higher cap, and a "high-stakes" mode at 0.95 for production/TOPIK prep. Keep same-day learning steps for first exposures. Grade effort and latency, not just correctness. Show intervals, not just "due."

## 3. Encoding and practice design

**Principle.** Encoding is stronger when learners generate, elaborate, and dual-code. But novices need worked examples before problem solving; the optimal amount of difficulty depends on expertise.

**Evidence.** The generation effect is d ≈ 0.40 across 86 studies ([Bertsch et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01483)); generating text beats reading it, g = 0.41 ([Schindler & Richter, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09758-w)). Worked examples beat problem solving, g = 0.48 ([Barbieri et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1)); the advantage reverses for experts (expertise reversal). Adding relevant pictures to an L1 gloss improves L2 vocabulary, g = 0.34–0.39 ([Zhang, Sala & Gobet, 2023](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/yge9n)); Mayer's multimedia principle is g = 0.68, but decorative or redundant visuals reduce learning ([Cromley & Chen, 2025](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-richard-mayers-multimedia-learning-research-se/)). Interleaving overall is g = 0.42, math g = 0.34, but blocking beats interleaving for word lists (g = −0.39; [Brunmair & Richter, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)).

**Design implication.** For grammar, use a four-step ladder: worked example → completion → generation → transfer. For vocabulary, show L2 word + audio + relevant image + L1 gloss; ban decorative art from cards. Interleave grammar contrasts and sound contrasts; block new vocabulary. Use "explain why" prompts only after a schema exists, and fade them.

## 4. Skill acquisition and language acquisition

**Principle.** Language skill moves from explicit declarative knowledge to proceduralized, automatic access through repeated meaningful use. Input is necessary but not sufficient; output, interaction, and noticing drive accuracy.

**Evidence.** Repeated practice after declarative study produces faster, less variable collocation processing—evidence for proceduralization and automatization ([SSLA study, 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/development-of-automaticity-in-processing-l2-collocations-the-roles-of-l1-collocational-knowledge-and-practice-condition/3DD70FC8D314667E1D663FCBF55E6464)). Krashen's input hypothesis claims i+1 is necessary and sufficient; it is unfalsifiable as stated and ignores attention, output, and negative feedback ([Krashen, 1985](https://www.sdkrashen.com/content/books/principles_and_practice.pdf); [2025 critique](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1636777/full)). Swain's output hypothesis holds that production pushes syntactic processing and noticing ([Swain, 1995](https://www.blackwellpublishing.com/content/BPL_images/Content_Store/WWW_Content/9780631217541/10Chap09.pdf)); Long's interaction hypothesis links negotiation for meaning to input, attention, and output ([Long, 1996](https://www.blackwellpublishing.com/content/BPL_images/Content_Store/WWW_Content/9780631217541/10Chap09.pdf)). Recasts produce g = 0.70 ([Rassaei, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/09571736.2022.2097298)); task-based teaching with focus on form produces better uptake and transfer than meaning-only tasks ([Chen & Lambert, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1075/task.00028.che)). Feedback timing per se has little average effect in computer-assisted learning (g = 0.03), but explicit, content-rich feedback does ([2026 meta](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10117-8)); in L2, immediate feedback is often equal or better ([Xu & Zeng, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1026174)). Practice should be transfer-appropriate: production tasks for speaking, listening tasks for listening, not recognition drills for conversation. Deliberate practice explains only ~14% of performance variance overall and ~5% in education ([Macnamara et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810)), so feedback quality and individualization beat raw hours. Incidental vocabulary learning yields only 9–18% of target words immediately and 6–17% delayed ([Webb et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0261444822000507)); repetition correlates r = .34 with learning ([Uchihara et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12343)).

**Design implication.** Every lesson needs four beats: comprehensible input at 95–98% coverage, a noticing task, pushed output, and feedback. Use task-based scenarios (ordering, directions, appointments) with a post-task "noticing recap" of 2–3 forms, then repeat the task. Teach high-frequency chunks (밥을 먹다, 전화를 하다, -아/어요, -습니다) as units. Do not rely on incidental exposure for core vocabulary; combine intentional FSRS cards with graded reading/listening.

## 5. Vocabulary and chunks

**Principle.** Vocabulary is the strongest single predictor of comprehension. Frequency and coverage should drive sequencing; chunks, not isolated words, are the unit of fluent use.

**Evidence.** Vocabulary knowledge correlates r = .57 with L2 reading and r = .56 with listening ([vocabulary–comprehension meta-analysis, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168820913998)). 95% coverage supports minimal comprehension; 98% supports adequate comprehension ([Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski, 2010](https://doi.org/10.64152/10125/66648)). A 2025 replication found 98% perceived as easier but no significant 90/95/98 differences on a 14-item test ([Webb et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.64152/10125/67517)), so coverage is a planning heuristic, not a law. Zipf's law means the top 1–2K lemmas carry most running text; Korean tokenization and particles change exact coverage, so Kiku must compute it from its own corpus. Receptive and productive knowledge are separable, and meaning recall correlates more strongly with reading while form recall correlates more strongly with listening ([vocabulary–comprehension meta-analysis, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168820913998)). Formulaic sequences are processed faster than matched novel strings (collocation effect ≈ 0.58; [Jiang & Siyanova-Chanturia, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12846)); explicit chunk instruction improves oral fluency ([formulaic-sequence instruction study, 2024](https://ejournals.ukm.my/gema/article/view/69569/0)).

**Design implication.** Sequence by frequency and coverage, not textbook topic. Build a Korean coverage meter: "this story is 96% known to you." Targets: 1,500 core words + 300 chunks for A2; 3,000–4,000 words for B1 reading; 6,000+ for unassisted news. Store chunks as single FSRS items with audio, image, and a production variant. Test recognition and production separately; never let multiple-choice recognition masquerade as mastery.

## 6. Phonology and perception

**Principle.** L2 perception is warped by L1 categories: learners initially show categorical perception through an L1 filter, and discrimination is worse near L1 prototypes (perceptual magnet effect). Perception can be trained, but generalization to production and new talkers is limited without explicit production practice.

**Evidence.** Discrimination is worse near L1 prototypes ([Kuhl, 1991](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03212211)). High-variability phonetic training (HVPT) improves perception, g = 0.67–0.92, and production, g = 0.49–0.66; multiple talkers are essential, with 5–8 a common optimum ([Uchihara et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0272263125100879); [Uchihara et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0142716424000195); [Mahdi & Mohsen, 2024](https://doi.org/10.32038/ltrq.2024.40.02)). Delayed production gains shrink to g = 0.26 and generalize only ~4.5% to untrained items. ASR-based pronunciation feedback yields g = 0.69, with explicit corrective feedback strongest ([Ngo et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344023000113)). Shadowing improves comprehensibility and prosody, but segmental evidence is inconclusive ([2025 review](https://doi.org/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827)). Audiovisual speech integrates with audio (McGurk effect; [McGurk & MacDonald, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1038/264746a0)); pitch gestures and visual cues help tone and intonation learning ([Farran & Morett, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2024.1410795)).

**Design implication.** Build a 3–5 minute "Sound Gym": minimal-pair identification for Korean's three-way stops (ㄱ/ㄲ/ㅋ, ㅂ/ㅃ/ㅍ, ㅈ/ㅉ/ㅊ) and vowels (ㅓ/ㅗ, ㅡ/ㅜ), with 5–8 voices and immediate feedback. Follow with record-and-compare shadowing and ASR feedback that names the feature ("aspirated ㅋ: more air"). Use mouth/tongue diagrams and pitch contours. Re-test at 1, 7, and 30 days. Target intelligibility and comprehensibility, not "native-like."

## 7. Motivation, habits, and affect

**Principle.** Sustained learning depends on autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Habits depend on stable cues and low friction. Anxiety and cognitive load suppress performance, especially in speaking.

**Evidence.** SDT meta-analyses support need satisfaction → engagement and persistence ([Ryan et al., 2023](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2023_RyanDuineveldDiDomenicoEtAl_Meta.pdf)); a 2025 L2 meta-analysis confirms the pattern ([Alamer et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10038-y)). Gamification has small-to-moderate effects on learning (g ≈ 0.25–0.49) and larger, less stable effects on motivation ([Sailer & Homner, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w); [Li, Hew & Du, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)). Controlling rewards can crowd out intrinsic motivation, and streaks can become "gamification misuse" ([Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3491140.3528274)). Implementation intentions work, d = .27–.66 ([Sheeran et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)); MCII is g = .34 ([MCII meta-analysis, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34054628/)). FL anxiety correlates r = −.33 to −.39 with performance ([Zhang, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12590)) and is associated with lower working-memory efficiency (θ = −.28; [2025 meta](https://doi.org/10.1080/09571736.2025.2532516)). Flow—challenge–skill balance, clear goals, immediate feedback—correlates r = .43 with achievement ([Zhang & Fang, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1270642)). Specific proximal goals and mastery experiences build self-efficacy, and self-efficacy is strongly negatively linked to anxiety (r = −.70; [2023 meta](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-022-04110-x)). Working memory is about four chunks ([Cowan, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922)); cognitive load theory predicts novices overload without worked examples ([Sweller, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9157-8)). Failure should be frequent enough to signal room to grow (~15%) but not so frequent that it defeats; that is the "desirable failure" zone.

**Design implication.** Give choice (topic, scenario, voice), mastery bars instead of global leaderboards, and a study buddy for relatedness. Make the minimum day 2 minutes. Use streak repair and vacation mode; never punitive hearts that block learning. Add an implementation-intention onboarding: "When [cue], I will do [2-minute Kiku] at [place]." Keep sessions 8–15 minutes, 60–80% review, one new chunk set, one production task. End with a specific win. For speaking, use private recording first, then low-stakes AI conversation; no public rankings for beginners.

## 8. Session design

**Principle.** Short, frequent, retrieval-heavy sessions beat long infrequent ones; the best session ends while the learner could still do one more.

**Evidence.** Microlearning meta-analyses report moderate-to-large effects (SMD ≈ 0.74) and favor 5–15-minute sessions, 3–5×/week, but the literature is heterogeneous and vulnerable to novelty effects ([2025 meta](https://doi.org/10.33365/jm.v7i2.517); [2024 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e41413)). Spacing and retrieval evidence implies review should dominate: ~60–70% review and 30–40% new early, moving to 80/20 as the deck matures. The 85% rule sets difficulty; sleep after learning improves declarative retention, and new material should not displace sleep.

**Design implication.** Default 10-minute "Daily Kiku": 1 warm-up retrieval, 3–5 interleaved review blocks, 1 new chunk set, 1 production task, 1 win. Offer a 2-minute "minimum day" and a 20–25-minute "deep day." Introduce each new item with audio, image, and text; put the hardest production early; schedule new material before 8pm; end on success. Test at 1, 7, 30, and 90 days (FSRS chooses exact intervals) and include a monthly unrehearsed transfer task.

## 9. Measurement and experimentation

**Principle.** Measure delayed, unrehearsed performance. Separate learning from engagement. Use the simplest model that supports decisions.

**Evidence.** CEFR and TOPIK are officially linked 1:1 (TOPIK 1–6 = A1–C2; [NIIED, 2023](https://www.topik.com.tw/upload/newsDoc/202401081118175oVO.pdf)). ACTFL–CEFR links are one-directional and modality-specific ([ACTFL](https://www.actfl.org/assessments/assigning-cefr-ratings-to-actfl-assessments)). BKT is interpretable and skill-specific ([Šarić-Grgić et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-023-09389-4)); DKT/SAKT can beat IRT on next-item prediction (AUC .77–.79 vs .74) but need large data and show low specificity ([EDM 2025](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.industry-papers.46/index.html)). FSRS predicts recall well but does not measure proficiency. Elo is a cheap, robust item/ability estimator for adaptive practice.

**Design implication.** Kiku's proficiency screen should report separate receptive/productive estimates mapped to TOPIK/CEFR, based on a monthly 10-minute assessment with unseen items and a 7-day delayed retest. Use FSRS for scheduling, Elo per item/skill for difficulty, and a simple BKT mastery flag per grammar pattern. Do not use DKT until >100k interactions. Dashboards should show "retained at 30 days," "can produce without cues," and "coverage of top 3K words," not XP. A/B tests must pre-register hypotheses, use learning outcomes as primary and engagement as a guardrail, and monitor anxiety, sleep, and churn. Never A/B test loss-framed streak mechanics or paywalls on vulnerable learners.

## The 15 highest-leverage design rules

1. **Retrieval by default.** Make production/cloze the default interaction; recognition is a scaffold, not the goal.
2. **FSRS-6 at 0.90 retention.** Expose the load forecast; let power users tune 0.85–0.95.
3. **Cap new items and review first.** 5–10 new items/day; reviews before new material.
4. **Target ~85% success.** Adapt within the session; below 75% add scaffolds, above 92% raise difficulty.
5. **Space, but don't over-engineer.** Expanding intervals are fine; uniform is not reliably worse.
6. **Interleave contrasts, block vocabulary.** Mix grammar/sound categories; keep new word sets blocked.
7. **Teach chunks as units.** Store collocations, speech-level endings, and honorific chunks as single cards.
8. **Worked example → faded practice.** For grammar, never start with cold production.
9. **Dual-code with discipline.** Relevant image + L1 gloss + audio; no decorative visuals.
10. **HVPT + production + ASR feedback.** 5–8 voices, minimal pairs, named phonetic feedback.
11. **8–15 minute sessions, 60–80% review.** One new chunk set, one production task, one win.
12. **Sleep-aware scheduling.** New material earlier; bedtime recall optional; no "sleep learning" claims.
13. **Support autonomy, competence, relatedness.** Choice, mastery bars, study buddies; no punitive streaks.
14. **Habit by design.** Implementation intentions + 2-minute minimum day + streak repair.
15. **Measure delayed transfer, with ethics guardrails.** Primary metric = unrehearsed 7/30-day performance.

## Myths to avoid

| Myth | What the evidence says | Kiku stance |
|---|---|---|
| "Learning styles" (visual/auditory/kinesthetic) | Most rigorous crossover tests find no meshing effect, yet ~89% of educators believe it ([Newton & Salvi, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.602451/full)). A 2024 meta reports a small g = 0.31, but "matching" is operationalized inconsistently ([2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11270031/)). | Teach every learner with multiple modalities; never diagnose a style. |
| Left-brain/right-brain learners | Hemispheric specialization exists for some functions, but "right-brained creativity" is not a learning type; 90.7% belief in one Mexican sample ([2024](http://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?lng=es&nrm=i.p&pid=S2007-48322024000100101&script=sci_arttext&tlng=en)). | No hemispheric branding or quizzes. |
| "We use only 10% of our brain" | False; most regions are active over a day. Still endorsed by 44.8% in the same sample. | Never repeat it, even as a hook. |
| Brain Gym / crossed-limb exercises | No credible evidence for interhemispheric "integration" improving learning; >50% of Turkish teachers endorsed it ([2023](https://educationandscience.ted.org.tr/article/view/2118)). | No motor-gimmick modules. |
| "Sleep learning" (play audio all night) | Sleep consolidates, and TMR can bias reactivation (g = 0.29), but complex new language is not learned passively during sleep. | Bedtime recall only; no overnight audio claims. |
| "Adults can't learn a language like children" | Adult brains remain plastic; structural changes appear in months; age mainly affects ultimate attainment ([van Hell, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12601)). | Market realistic progress, not childhood fatalism. |
| "i+1 is a precise, sufficient input level" | i+1 is not operationalized; input is necessary but not sufficient; output, interaction, and form focus add value. | Use coverage targets and noticing tasks, not a mystical "i+1." |
| "Grammar doesn't matter; just input" | Form-focused instruction and recasts produce medium effects (recasts g = 0.70). | Teach form in meaning-focused tasks. |
| "Native-like accent is the goal" | Intelligibility/comprehensibility matter more; HVPT production gains fade without maintenance. | Target clear, comfortable speech. |
| "Gamification = learning" | Effects on learning are small-to-moderate; motivation effects are larger but unstable; misuse distracts from learning ([Hadi Mogavi et al., 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3491140.3528274)). | Game the habit, not the grade. |
| "A long streak proves learning" | Streaks exploit loss aversion and can lift short-term return (company data: +1.7% 7-day retention from a streak animation), but they do not prove durable learning ([Duolingo, 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)). | Streak = consistency; learning = delayed test. |
| "10,000 hours / deliberate practice explains expertise" | Deliberate practice explains ~14% of variance overall and ~5% in education ([Macnamara et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810)); quality and individualization matter more than hours. | Optimize feedback and adaptivity, not raw time. |
| "More study time is always better" | Massed practice is inferior; cognitive load and sleep limit returns. | Cap sessions; protect sleep; measure retention per minute. |

*Practical bottom line: Kiku should be a retrieval-and-feedback machine wrapped in a humane habit loop, with FSRS underneath, chunks and coverage above, and delayed production tests as the only honest scoreboard.*
